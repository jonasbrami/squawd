## Your four questions

**Q1 — PX4 land detection in SITL.** The multicopter detector is `LNDMC_*`, not `LNDFW_*` (fixed-wing) and not `MPC_LAND_*` (auto-land descent speed). v1.15 defaults: `LNDMC_TRIG_TIME` 1.0 s, `LNDMC_Z_VEL_MAX` 0.25 m/s, `LNDMC_XY_VEL_MAX` 1.5 m/s, `LNDMC_ROT_MAX` 20 °/s, `LNDMC_ALT_GND` 2 m (ground-effect band). Two things matter for you: (a) there is **no altitude trigger** — land detection is a low-thrust + low-motion condition, so it engaging at ~0.5 m physical means the vehicle had already effectively touched down / stopped demanding lift. It is a *symptom* of the frozen-bias descent, confirming codex's ordering. (b) The "climb setpoints ignored" latch is almost certainly `COM_DISARM_LAND` (default **2.0 s**, `commander_params.c:209`): after land detection the commander auto-disarms, and a disarmed vehicle ignores offboard setpoints. The legitimate, documented SITL mitigation is `COM_DISARM_LAND 0` for the demo so a ground graze isn't terminal — do **not** gut the `LNDMC_*` thresholds; that would be masking. The cleaner mechanism for holding 1.2 m: there is no honest PX4-side one with this fixed sensor suite (no downward rangefinder → `EKF2_HGT_REF=2` (range) unavailable; terrain estimator needs the same). The product-honest physical anchor you already have is the camera elevation servo; the sim-config-honest fix is resolving the EKF-z divergence at its source.

**Q2 — is 1.2 m co-altitude the right geometry?** Keep mov_1 as the gate — switching to mov_0 after the gate was framed around mov_1 would look like (and be) gate redefinition, which your own constraints forbid. But the trade is real and worth using diagnostically: mov_1 gives ±0.6 m vertical margin (1.2 m box) but stacks three ground-coupled hazards (land-detect/disarm is an *absorbing* failure state, ToF ground returns during pitch transients, box-bottom edge-mix) on top of the quantity under test; mov_0 at z≈10 m gives a 3× tighter ±0.2 m band but a sky background (clean OUT_OF_RANGE, easier 0-false-assoc) and — critically — *recoverable* misses: a 1 m sink puts the box at +5.7° elevation at 10 m, comfortably inside the ±21° vertical FOV, so the servo pulls you back instead of PX4 disarming you. Run mov_0 as an isolation experiment to separate fusion quality from ground-stack effects; don't substitute it. **Elevation-servo target per box height:** your −3.4° is `−atan((h/2)/R)` for h=1.2, R=10 — correct, but hardcoded. Generalize to `target_el = −degrees(atan2(h/2, R))` using the fused range when RANGE_LOCKED (else assumed standoff): mov_0 (h=0.4) needs −1.1° at 10 m / −1.4° at 8 m, and your current −3.4° would command the drone ~0.4–0.6 m *below* mov_0's centre — half its band.

**Q3 — flaws in the control reasoning.** The `off_d` recomputation is a red herring you should simplify: `me[2]` is `−lp.z` by construction (`ned_to_enu`, `agents/world/model.py:73`), so `off_d = lp.z + me[2] ≡ 0` identically, and the commanded z is just `−alt` in the EKF frame (likewise `off_n = −i·spacing`, constant). It's correct but obfuscated, and it makes the real property explicit: **every setpoint is EKF-frame with no physical anchor except the elevation servo**. Real issues, in order: (a) the EKF-frame `max(0.5, …)` altitude floor is illusory under drift (claim 2 above); (b) blind recovery (`ops.py:125-140`) only sweeps *yaw*, but your measured blind cause is *vertical* — the box exits the FOV top after sinking; no yaw sweep recovers that. Add a slow bounded climb bias during blind (sinking is the only observed direction); (c) `lp` is None-guarded for `own_sp` (line 84) but dereferenced unguarded at lines 112/136 — a telemetry hiccup crashes `_acquire`; (d) the envelope's `speed_max_mps=3.0` vs shadow speed: at radial standoff −8 m inside a 35 m orbit the drone moves ~3.5·27/35 ≈ 2.7 m/s — you pass the gate with ~0.3 m/s margin, and any standoff geometry error or catch-up transient drops fusion availability against your ≥80% gate. Check this in the availability telemetry before blaming association; (e) the drift EMA (α=0.3 at 1 Hz) mixes braking/maneuver transients of EKF-z into a "bias" estimate — a slower filter or ramp fit would be more faithful, but see Q4 item 3 first.
